# Verified source findings used for v2

**Source-review cutoff:** 18 October 2026. This is a working audit of verified source statements for the report, not a substitute for the source files. Restricted or gated data were not downloaded and no access controls were bypassed.

## REMA 2022 source report

- Official REMA-hosted PDF: https://www.rema.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/REMA/Publications/Reports/Report_2_-_Landslide_Risks_Assessment_and_Mitigation_Report_3.pdf. GitHub mirror named in the task: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/theoneste2060/rwanda_landslide_inventory/main/Report_2_-_Landslide_Risks_Assessment_and_Mitigation_Report_3.pdf. The official PDF was downloaded and all 68 pages were extracted; image-heavy tables/figures were rendered for visual verification.
- PDF p. 9 (printed p. 8): Google Earth visual mapping; image resolution 30–60 cm; image dates 2000–2021 for Step 1; field photographs alongside; shallow definition estimated depth <5 m.
- PDF p. 10 (printed p. 9): 22 old deep-seated landslides and 5 recent deep-seated flow slides/earthflows in four sub-catchments. Recent is between two satellite-image dates; old is visible in the oldest imagery without activity.
- PDF p. 11 (printed p. 10): no shallow failures were identified in the four catchments in the preceding 20 years, but authors do not infer none occurred; small landslides can be missed/obscured. Regional clusters in Lake Kivu can contain hundreds to thousands over a few (tenths of) km².
- PDF p. 12 (printed p. 11): creep and gully mapping is not comprehensive; road failures may be too small to identify in Google Earth.
- PDF p. 14 (printed p. 13): report says the local inventory is too limited for data-driven susceptibility/hazard at the four small catchments; a broader western-Rwanda dataset was used in addition.
- PDF p. 15 (printed p. 14): the 2,544 recent shallow observations were visually mapped in Google Earth over 2000–2019; each represented by one manually set initiation point/pixel; impact zone excluded from susceptibility; average total area 725 m², source 121 m², impact 604 m². Authors assert common Google Earth detection underestimation and assume its similarity across the region.
- PDF p. 16 (printed p. 15): Figure 3 shows 2,544 shallow landslides and 83 knickpoints.
- PDF p. 17 (printed p. 16), Table 1: terrain predictors from DEM at 10/30 m; PGA 2.2 km; rejuvenated landscape 1:1,000; land cover GGGI/RWB; roads RTDA; six land-cover classes and five lithology classes.
- PDF p. 19 (printed p. 18), Table 2: 2,544 positives and an equal number of randomly sampled non-landslide points outside landslide polygons; forward stepwise logistic regression; random 10-fold CV. AUC 10 m: 89.3% with geology, 85.0% without; 30 m: 91.1% with, 88.8% without. Text selects 30 m without geology due to coarse lithology and marginal difference.
- PDF p. 20 (printed p. 19), Figure 4: univariate AUCs: slope 88.9%, elevation 63.1%, planar curvature 58.4%, fault distance 60.0%, river distance 59.0%, land cover 55.2%, road distance 53.5%, PGA 54.1%, east exposure 53.0%, rejuvenated landscape 52.9%, clay 51.1%, north exposure 50.4%; profile curvature has no legible labelled score.
- PDF p. 25–27 (printed pp. 24–26): hazard is annualized source-area rate by susceptibility class over ~20 years. Table 6 class rates 365, 90, 18, and 1 m²/year/km² for the 0–10%, >10–30%, >30–60%, >60–100% susceptibility classes. PDF p. 27 contains authors’ ~2,700-year and 0.2%/1% impact interpretation; the implied correction to 1% is qualitative and not quantitatively calibrated in the report.
- PDF p. 28–31 (printed pp. 27–30): exposure is a desktop overlap of hazard classes with current 2018 and planned 2050 land-use classes. Report says vulnerability databases were not received. Table categories differ between scenario years. Names vary between “Rusizi” and “Kamembe-Gihundwe” and “Magerwa”/“Rwandex-Magerwa”.
- PDF p. 54 (printed p. 53): CROM DSS uses RUSLE for sheet/rill erosion, not gullying or landslides; report cautions that erosion controls can increase infiltration and regolith water content. PDF p. 58 (printed p. 57): no field measurements; mitigation is assumptions-based, targets hazard rates 365 and 90, assumes 30% reforestation and 500 m trenches/ditches per 3 ha. PDF p. 61 (printed p. 60): measures by sub-catchment; bill-of-quantities cost columns are blank.

## Public and gated inventory sources

- The supplied source registry labels two rows `depicker_2020a` and `depicker_2020b`, but both resolve to the same Geomorphology article DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geomorph.2019.106886. Treat these as duplicate bibliographic aliases, not two papers or two datasets. The article is “The added value of a regional landslide susceptibility assessment: the western branch of the East African Rift” (Geomorphology 353, 106886) and describes a 6,446-landslide catalogue, including 29% deep-seated landslides and 198 larger than 5 ha. Publisher/institutional records were checked; the exact 6,446-record primary file, schema and dataset license were not verified as publicly downloadable.
- Depicker et al. (2021), Earth Surface Dynamics 9, 445–462, DOI https://doi.org/10.5194/esurf-9-445-2021. This is a distinct article from the 2019 Geomorphology paper. Official full text and supplement were opened; the article is CC BY 4.0. The associated 7,944-point shallow-landslide update is openly deposited at Zenodo 5027004 under CC BY 4.0, but it is not confirmed to be identical to the 6,446 catalogue or the REMA 2,544-point subset.
- Dewitte et al. (2021), Landslides, DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/s10346-020-01452-0. Publisher page opened. It describes a Google Earth inventory update, >9,000 landslides in the broader work, shallow source polygons and manually defined initiation points for the shallow subset; image acquisitions 2000–2019 and timing between images. The paper’s data statement points readers to the PAStECA project/public online release.
- Depicker et al. (2021), “Historical dynamics of landslide risk from population and forest-cover changes in the Kivu Rift”, Nature Sustainability 4, 965–974, DOI https://doi.org/10.1038/s41893-021-00757-9. Correct DOI resolved and publisher full text opened. Its data-availability section directly links the landslide inventory to Zenodo DOI https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.5027004 and source data figures to Figshare DOI https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.14838825. The Nature page names corresponding authors Arthur Depicker (KU Leuven) and Olivier Dewitte (RMCA).
- Zenodo record https://zenodo.org/records/5027004, DOI https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.5027004. Official record/API opened. It contains a public shapefile with 7,944 point geometries for 2000–2019 in the North Tanganyika–Kivu region, licensed CC BY 4.0. The archive was downloaded and checksum-verified against the Zenodo API. DBF fields are `ID`, `area`, `dep_area`, `Year`; point geometry supplies coordinates. There are 1,259 negative signed Year values, 6,685 positive values, zero blanks and 34 distinct signed values; the archive supplies no field dictionary that explains the negative sign, so do not use raw Year values as calendar years until the authors clarify the encoding. The record contains no exact image-date pair or explicit landslide-type column. Article text says one manually identified initiation point and source-area polygons per landslide, but the field semantics/units of `area` and `dep_area` need author confirmation. This public 7,944-region file is not yet confirmed as the exact 2,544-point input version used in the REMA report.
- The Earth Surface Dynamics article explicitly says the image-range (oldest-to-youngest image) varies by location and corrects frequency/erosion-rate estimates for variable imagery density; it notes more imagery in urban areas and some areas with no imagery. The point file itself has no image-footprint/range/density fields. The 2022 REMA report instead describes a general ~20-year denominator and an assumption of similar under-detection across the regional inventory. Those are different documented treatments and should not be conflated.
- Monsieurs et al. (2018), DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/s10346-018-1008-y. Springer landing page opened. Abstract describes LIWEAR as 143 landslide events with known location and date across 1968–2016. Publisher lists supplementary material as available to authorized users. No unrestricted event-level shapefile/CSV or reuse license was verified here; do not bypass the publisher restriction.
- Monsieurs et al. (2019), NHESS 19, 775–797, DOI https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-19-775-2019. Full open NHESS article opened; CC BY 4.0 article license. It documents 174 events 2001–2018, daily-accuracy dates and mean location accuracy 6.7 km; 29 mining events excluded, 143 used for threshold calibration; authors explicitly do not claim catalogue completeness. Supplement is public, but in this review no separate row-level occurrence geometry file/license was identified. Article data routes must be confirmed before treating it as open event data.
- Figure source data for the Nature Sustainability paper: Figshare DOI https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.14838825.v1, record opened and API confirmed CC BY 4.0. It is an Excel workbook for figures 3–5, not the underlying per-landslide spatial inventory.
- RMCA geocatalogue https://geocatalogue.africamuseum.be/ opened; the current page says the catalog contains no information/layers. No separate public RMCA/Ghent event catalogue was verified there.

## Method evidence

- Peduzzi et al. (1996), “A simulation study of the number of events per variable in logistic regression analysis”, DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/S0895-4356(96)00236-3. DOI/publisher route and PubMed search record checked; publisher full-text route required a login. Use it only as a historically influential simulation motivating an events-per-variable caution, not as a universal rule.
- Roberts et al. (2017), “Cross-validation strategies for data with temporal, spatial, hierarchical, or phylogenetic structure”, DOI https://doi.org/10.1111/ecog.02881. Full publisher text opened. It explains that random folds can underestimate prediction error under spatial dependence and that blocking should match the prediction target/dependence scale.
- Rolain et al. (2025), “Random cross-validation produces biased assessment of machine learning performance in regional landslide susceptibility prediction”, DOI https://doi.org/10.3390/rs17020213. Full MDPI article opened. It reports random-CV assessments higher than spatial-CV in its own study and higher variability under spatial validation; numerical changes are study-specific and not transferable to Rwanda.
- Uwihirwe et al. (2020), DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/s10346-020-01457-9, full publisher article sections opened. It uses 59 MPRE and 60 daily conditions from the Rwanda catalogue and reports high TPR with false positives for fitted thresholds; not an independent prospective test.
- Riveros et al. (2022), DOI https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-22-3641-2022, full NHESS article opened. It describes 55 accurately dated landslides (2007–2019), 32 in modeled catchments, 5–25 km spatial accuracy, and explicitly acknowledges impact-reporting bias and small sample size; the paper’s AUC and TPR/FPR claims are tied to the study-specific setup.

## Additional dataset records inspected during final extraction

- **4TU.ResearchData event workbook snapshot.** The included workbook has one worksheet with 71 rows × 35 columns (header + 70 source rows). Its bundled README says the landslide inventory covers 2006–2021; the dataset includes separate groundwater data from 2016–2021. Inspected fields include `event_id`, raw `event_date`, `event_time`, `event_title`, `event_description`, `location_description`, `location_accuracy`, `landslide_category`, `landslide_trigger`, `landslide_size`, `landslide_setting`, `fatality_count`, `injury_count`, `latitude`, `longitude`, administrative place names, gazetteer distance, source links and imported-source IDs. Coordinates and location-accuracy values vary; this is an event catalogue, not initiation-scar geometry. The upstream raw-source manifest identifies the 4TU record as CC0; the packaged file is the repository snapshot and the exact official record locator was not established in this review. Bundled README gives J. Uwihirwe contact: J.Uwihirwe@tudelft.nl.
- **Zenodo 7198322 / Deijns et al. SAR-timed geomorphic-hazard inventory.** Official Zenodo API record identifies a public CC BY 4.0 dataset describing four regional event inventories with around 2,500 mapped landslides and flash floods in total, including Karongi, Rwanda. The Rwanda shapefile snapshot inspected contains 494 polygon records in WGS 84 / UTM 35S. Its DBF fields are `id`, `Area`, `LandUsemaj`, `Aspectmean`, `Slopemean`, `ASC_MASKma`, `DSC_MASKma`, and `Aspect`. It contains no date, image bracket, initiation point, or explicit per-feature landslide-vs-flash-flood class. Do not use it as a pure landslide training layer without classification and timing reconciliation.
- **REMA Figure 4 / Appendix 1.** Figure 4 uses single-variable logistic AUC as its importance measure. Visible labeled scores: slope 88.9%, elevation 63.1%, planar curvature 58.4%, fault distance 60.0%, river distance 59.0%, land cover 55.2%, road presence 53.5%, PGA 54.1%, east exposure 53.0%, rejuvenated landscape 52.9%, clay 51.1%, north exposure 50.4%; profile-curvature score has no legible label. Table 10 VIFs for continuous predictors range 1.00–3.46, below the report’s VIF >4 flag; Table 11 condition-index interpretation uses >30 as concerning and reports no problematic multicollinearity. These checks do not address spatial leakage or external prediction.
- **REMA Table 7 / exposure tables.** Table 7 exact site-area percentages and km² by hazard class, Table 8 current 2018 exposure, and Table 9 planned 2050 exposure are reproduced row-by-row in `rema_extraction_table.csv`. Table 8/9 exposure columns are color-coded; the class order (1, 18, 90, 365 m²/year/km²) was checked against the rate legend on PDF page 28. The report’s page 27 printed-page text derives 0.2% from 6×365 m² per km², then says about 1% after underestimation without a calibrated detection correction.
